Privacy Policy

Effective date: 28 September 2026

This privacy policy applies to the Android app named at the top of this page (“the app”), published on Google Play by Levent Mert. On your phone, the app’s name may be shown translated into your language. This policy explains, in plain words, what the app uses on your phone, what leaves your phone, and what your choices are.

In short

  • Your contacts, call history, phone numbers and names stay on your phone. The app never uploads them. Two exceptions, both explained below: the caller’s name is passed to your phone’s own text-to-speech app so it can be read aloud (section 2), and a “Report a problem” e-mail that you choose to send includes a list of recent call events – without any names or numbers (section 5).
  • The app has no servers, no user accounts and no analytics of its own.
  • The only parts of the app that use the internet are Google’s advertising tools built into it: the advertising banner at the bottom of the home screen and the ad-consent check that comes with it. Google collects some technical device data through them (see section 4).
  • If you write to us for help, we see only what you decide to send.

1. Who we are

The app is made by Levent Mert, an individual developer based in Türkiye, publishing with a personal Google Play developer account under the developer name levottin. There is no company behind the app.

Privacy contact: bigclear.destek@gmail.com

For the support e-mails described in section 5, Levent Mert is the data controller (under the EU/UK GDPR) and the veri sorumlusu (under Türkiye’s personal data protection law, KVKK).

2. What the app uses on your phone, and why

The app itself processes everything in this section only on your phone. Where another service is involved – your phone’s speech recognition or text-to-speech, your phone’s Contacts app, your contacts account sync, or your mobile network – this is pointed out below. The app can use your contacts, call history, phone functions and notifications only after you allow it – either by answering Android’s permission question, or by choosing the app as your default phone app, which gives a phone app these permissions automatically. You can see and change these permissions at any time in your phone’s Settings → Apps → the app → Permissions.

Contacts (read)

To show the name and photo of the person calling you, your favourites and the contacts list, to find a contact when you search by typing or by voice, and to check whether a caller is in your contacts (for the “Silence unknown callers” option).

Contacts (write) – only two small changes

When you ask it to, the app can (1) mark or unmark a contact as a favourite (the same “star” your Contacts app uses) and (2) choose which of a contact’s numbers a favourite button calls (the contact’s “default number”). Adding, editing, and deleting contacts is done in your phone’s own Contacts app. When you add a number to your contacts (from Recent calls, or right after a call), the app opens your phone’s Contacts app with the number, and the name you typed or said, already filled in; you save it there. If your contacts are synced with an account (for example your Google account), your phone syncs these two changes as it normally does; the app itself sends nothing.

Call history (read)

To show your recent calls (the latest 40) on the Recent calls screen, and to choose which of a favourite’s numbers to call, based on the numbers you actually talked to in the last 180 days. The app uses this only while it is your default phone app. It reads your call history on the phone each time and does not copy it anywhere.

Phone and calls

As your default phone app, the app places and receives your calls, shows the incoming call screen, lets you choose a SIM card on dual-SIM phones, and reads the country of your SIM card (or, if that is unknown, of the mobile network) so the emergency button calls the right number (112 or 911). Like any phone app, the number you call goes to your mobile network operator to connect the call. The app does not record calls, and it does not read your own phone number or your phone’s serial numbers (such as the IMEI).

The app also checks, on the phone only, whether airplane mode, a missing or locked SIM card, no mobile network, silent or vibrate mode, Do Not Disturb or a low battery could stop your calls, and shows a warning on the home screen. The app does not send this information anywhere; only when you tap TURN SOUND ON does the recent events list note whether it worked (section 5).

Notifications

To show incoming calls over the lock screen (full-screen notification) and to show a notification during an ongoing call.

Voice search and saying a name (microphone)

The app does not have microphone permission. When you tap a voice button – to find a contact, or to say the name of a new contact you are adding – the app opens your phone’s own speech recognition service (for example Google’s). That service listens and turns your speech into text under its own privacy terms, and it may process the audio online. The app sends it only the language and a fixed question, never names or numbers. The app receives only the recognised text. It uses it to find a contact on your phone or, when you are adding a contact, as the new contact’s name, which it passes to your phone’s Contacts app. The app does not keep the text itself.

Reading the caller’s name aloud

The sentence to be spoken, with the caller’s name, is passed to the text-to-speech engine installed on your phone (for example Speech Services by Google) so that it can say “Anna is calling”. If the caller is not in your contacts, it says “Unknown number” – it does not read out the digits. The text-to-speech engine is a separate app with its own privacy terms and, depending on its settings, may use the internet. This feature is on when you install the app; you can turn it off in the app’s Settings (“Say who is calling”).

Vibration, flashlight and proximity sensor

For strong vibration and flashlight blinking when a call comes in, and to turn the screen off when the phone is at your ear. The app only switches the flashlight on and off; it never takes pictures or uses the camera image.

Your settings

Your choices (text size, order of favourites, on/off switches, and the time of your last emergency call – used so that “silence unknown callers” does not silence an emergency service calling you back for one hour) are saved in the app’s private storage on your phone. So is a short list of numbers you called that did not connect: for each one, the last 10 digits of the number and how many times in a row the call failed. It is used to hide the “CALL AGAIN” button after repeated failures and to skip a favourite’s number that keeps failing, and a number is removed from it as soon as you call it and actually get through (a call that the other side ends within 10 seconds, such as an operator announcement, does not count). A short list of recent app events and the details of the last crash are kept there too, used only for the “Report a problem” e-mail (sections 5 and 6).

All permissions, at a glance

  • Contacts (read, write), call history (read): see above.
  • Phone (make calls, phone state), notifications, full-screen alerts, phone-call foreground service: to make and receive calls, show the call screen and call notification, and warn you on the home screen when something (such as no network or a locked SIM) could stop calls.
  • Vibration and keep awake: for the strong vibration alert and to turn the screen off at your ear during a call.
  • Internet, network state, advertising ID and Android’s ad-services permissions: used only by Google’s advertising and consent tools (section 4).

The app does not have permission to use the microphone, the camera, your location (GPS or network location), SMS messages or your files. Google’s advertising tools may still estimate an approximate location from your IP address (section 4).

3. What the app never does

  • It never uploads your contacts, call history, phone numbers, names, voice or photos, and never sends them to advertisers or to anyone else. The only thing from your call history that can reach the developer is the list of recent call events – without names or numbers – inside a “Report a problem” e-mail that you choose to send (section 5). (Inside your phone, the app does hand the caller’s name to your phone’s own text-to-speech engine so it can be read aloud, and a new contact’s number and name to your Contacts app when you add one – see section 2.)
  • It does not record calls.
  • It has no servers of its own, no user accounts and no log-in.
  • It contains no analytics or crash-tracking tools of its own (such as Firebase Analytics or Crashlytics).
  • It does not ask for location permission.
  • The developer does not sell your personal data. What Google’s advertising tools collect is described in section 4.

4. Advertising – the only data the app itself sends automatically

The app shows one advertising banner at the bottom of the home screen. Ads never appear on the incoming call, ongoing call, contact card or emergency screens. The ads are provided by Google AdMob through the Google Mobile Ads SDK, which is built into the app, together with Google’s consent tool (the User Messaging Platform). Each time the app is opened, the consent tool contacts Google to check whether a consent form is needed where you are; this uses the same kind of technical data as the ads (for example your IP address and advertising ID).

According to Google, the ads SDK automatically collects and shares with Google:

  • IP address, which may be used to estimate the general location of the device;
  • app and ad interactions, such as app launches, taps and ad views;
  • diagnostic information about the performance of the app and the SDK (for example launch time, hang rate, energy use);
  • device identifiers: the Android advertising ID, the app set ID and, if applicable, other identifiers related to accounts signed in on the device.

To show and measure ads, Google and other advertising companies may place and read cookies or similar small files and identifiers in the app’s storage on your phone, and may use web beacons and your IP address to collect information as a result of ad serving. Google’s tools also keep your consent choice there.

Google uses this data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. It is encrypted in transit (TLS). Through AdMob, an ad may also come from another advertising company that buys ad space via Google; Google decides which companies take part and what they receive under its own rules (in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland they are listed in the consent form). The app does not give Google’s advertising tools or any advertising company your contacts, call history, phone numbers or names. The developer does not receive this data; AdMob only shows the developer summary reports (for example the number of ads shown and earnings).

For these tools, the app includes the internet, network state and advertising ID permissions, and Android’s ad-services permissions (used for Android’s own ad-privacy features, only on phones that still offer them).

Legal basis under the GDPR (EEA, UK and Switzerland): the choice you make in Google’s consent form (Art. 6(1)(a)); if you do not agree, Google may still show ads that are not personalised, relying on legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) as described in its own policies. Google acts as an independent controller of the advertising data it collects.

How Google handles this data: Google Privacy Policy · How Google uses information from apps that use its services · Google Mobile Ads SDK data disclosure · Google’s list of ad technology providers.

Your choices about ads

  • European Economic Area, United Kingdom and Switzerland: before any ad is loaded, the app shows Google’s consent form, where you choose whether Google and its partners may use your data for personalised ads. Your choice is stored on your phone. You can change it at any time with the “Ad privacy options” button in the app’s Settings. The button appears only where Google’s consent tool offers this choice – which may also include other places where the law gives you a similar choice (for example some US states).
  • Everywhere: you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone’s Settings (usually under Privacy → Ads, or Google → Ads), and manage ad personalisation at My Ad Center.
  • Without an internet connection no ads are loaded; calling works as usual.

If a paid option to remove ads is offered later, the payment will be handled entirely by Google Play, and the developer will not see your payment details. This policy will be updated before that happens.

5. When you contact us for help

The “Report a problem” button in the app’s Settings opens your own e-mail app with a message already addressed to bigclear.destek@gmail.com. Nothing reaches us until you press Send (your e-mail app may keep an unsent draft in your own mailbox, as it does with any e-mail you start). You can read, change or delete any part of the message first. (If your phone has no e-mail app, Android lets you pick another app to send the message with; the same applies.)

The prepared message contains only:

  • the app version and build code;
  • the phone manufacturer and model, and the Android version;
  • the app language, and whether the app is currently the default phone app;
  • the app’s settings (for example text size, and which options are switched on or off), whether notifications and full-screen call alerts are allowed, on Xiaomi/Redmi/POCO phones whether “Show on lock screen” and “Display pop-up windows while running in the background” are allowed, which permissions (contacts, call history, phone) the app has been given, and whether ads are switched on and allowed by the consent choice;
  • the most recent app events (at most 40) with the time they happened – for example “ringing”, “answered”, “ended”; for each call, whether it was incoming or outgoing, how long you talked (in seconds), how it ended (for example missed, rejected, busy or not reachable, with Android’s technical code and short reason) and whether the caller was in your contacts (yes/no); whether a call was silenced; presses of buttons such as “call”, “call again”, “add to contacts” and “turn sound on” (and whether the phone was still silent afterwards); and error codes of the ad consent form. No names and no phone numbers are written into this list; as an extra safety measure, runs of 7 or more digits that look like phone numbers are masked.
  • the technical details of the last crash, if there was one (see section 6).

When you send it, we also receive your e-mail address, the sender name your e-mail app shows with it, and anything you write or attach. Please do not include other people’s numbers or details you do not want to share; screenshots may show names or numbers. The e-mail is carried by your e-mail provider and arrives in the developer’s Gmail mailbox (a Google service).

We use support e-mails only to answer you and to fix problems in the app. We do not use them for marketing and do not share or sell them. Legal basis: under the GDPR, handling your request (Art. 6(1)(b)) and our legitimate interest in fixing the app (Art. 6(1)(f)); under KVKK, Art. 5(2)(f) (legitimate interest).

6. Crashes

If the app closes unexpectedly, the technical details of the crash (the time, the app version and which part of the program failed) are saved in the app’s private storage on your phone. These details are not meant to contain names or numbers, but a technical error message can occasionally include a piece of the information the app was handling at that moment. The app masks runs of digits that look like phone numbers, but this cannot catch every format or a name – which is why you see the full text in the e-mail and can delete it before sending. Only the latest crash is kept. The next time you open the app, it asks whether you want to report it; if you agree, the e-mail described in section 5 is prepared. Nothing is sent automatically.

Google Play vitals: if you installed the app from Google Play and allowed your phone to share usage and diagnostics data with Google (an Android setting), Google may provide the developer with crash and “app not responding” statistics and technical crash reports through the Google Play Console. This is controlled by your Android settings and covered by Google’s privacy policy; these reports do not include your contacts or call history.

7. How long data is kept, and how to delete it

  • On your phone: your settings, the list of numbers whose calls did not connect, the recent events list and the last crash report stay in the app’s private storage until you uninstall the app or clear its storage (Settings → Apps → the app → Storage → Clear storage). Google’s advertising and consent tools keep their own small files in the same storage (for example your consent choice, cookies and cached ads); these are removed the same way. Your contacts and call history belong to your phone and are not deleted when you uninstall the app; favourite stars and default numbers you set stay in your contacts.
  • Android backup: if backup is turned on on your phone, Android may include the app’s settings (text size, switches, order of favourites, time of your last emergency call) and the small files Google’s advertising and consent tools keep in the app’s storage (for example your consent choice) in your own phone backup (for example in your Google account), as it does for most apps. The recent events list, the crash report and the list of numbers whose calls did not connect are never included in the backup or in a transfer to a new phone. This backup belongs to you; the developer cannot see it. You control it in your phone’s backup settings.
  • Support e-mails: kept only as long as needed to handle your request, and deleted no later than 12 months after the last message of the conversation, unless the law requires otherwise. You can ask us to delete your e-mails at any time by writing to bigclear.destek@gmail.com; we will delete them within 30 days and confirm.
  • Advertising data: kept by Google according to Google’s privacy policy. The developer cannot see or delete it; you can manage it in your Google settings and your phone’s ad settings (you can reset or delete your advertising ID there, see section 4).

8. Security

Data the app keeps is stored in its private storage, which Android protects from other apps. Because the app has no servers, there is no copy of your contacts or call history with us that could leak (apart from the call events without names or numbers in support e-mails you choose to send, section 5). Google encrypts the advertising SDK’s traffic in transit. Support e-mails are kept in a personal e-mail account that only the developer can access. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, but we keep the data we hold to the minimum.

9. Children

The app is designed for adults, especially older people. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children. The ads in the app are not set up for children. If you believe a child has sent us personal data, write to us and we will delete it.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live – for example under the GDPR in the EU/EEA and the UK, or under Türkiye’s law no. 6698 (KVKK) – you have the right to know whether we hold data about you, to get a copy, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to or restrict its use, to withdraw consent, and to complain to your data protection authority (in Türkiye: Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurumu).

  • Data on your phone is fully in your hands: clear the app’s storage or uninstall the app.
  • For support e-mails, write to bigclear.destek@gmail.com. We reply within 30 days.
  • For advertising data, use the “Ad privacy options” button (where shown), your phone’s ad settings and Google’s tools linked in section 4.

Google may process advertising data, and Gmail stores support e-mails, in countries other than the one you live in (for example the United States), under Google’s own safeguards.

11. Changes to this policy

If the app changes the way it handles data, we will update this page and its effective date before the change reaches you. Important changes will also be mentioned in the app’s update notes on Google Play.
